About

Why PromptCollab exists

Good prompts are rarely written by one person. PromptCollab gives a team one place to write them together.

The problem

When a team works with AI, the prompt usually lives in one person's chat window. Everyone else sends ideas over Slack, someone pastes them in by hand, and the same request gets sent five times with small changes. It's slow, it wastes money on half-finished requests, and nobody can see what was actually sent.

The idea

PromptCollab treats a prompt like a shared document. Everyone in a room edits the same text at the same time, picks a model together, and reads the answer together. The conversation stays in the room, so the next person can pick up where the last one left off.

Who it's for

Developers, students and small teams who use AI models together: writing code with an assistant, drafting docs, or testing how different models answer the same question.

Where it stands

PromptCollab is an early project, in private preview. I'm talking to developers to learn what works, what's confusing and what's missing, and the next features will come from those conversations. Ideas being explored include comparing models side by side, prompt version history, and shared team keys with spending limits.

Who's behind it
